    How to read this

    The big figure is the share of a process's time with AI involved, which means every task that is not Human-led. The thick bar shows your choices and the thin bar shows our starting view. The same measure is used on every process. Hours are not added up across processes, because each process counts them in its own way, such as per week or per joiner. The numbers come from the choices saved in this browser. A process you have not changed shows our starting view. This is a starting picture to test and discuss, not a measurement of your team.

    Labels and where work is delivered

    Each label also points to where the work should sit in an HR service delivery model. As a task moves from Human-led to Augment to Automate it moves down the tiers, toward self-service. Create opens new services at either end.

    • Human-ledTier 2–3 · specialists, CoE & HRBPKeep it with experts and partners.
    • AugmentTier 1–2 · assisted agents & specialistsKeep it with people, but AI makes them faster.
    • AutomateTier 0–1 · self-service & automated workflowPush it to a portal or bot; people spot-check.
    • CreateNew services · at any tierNew self-service experiences, or new strategic advisory.
    Two things to keep in mind

    Human-led means a person leads, not that the work is senior: a sensitive call at a shared-service desk is human-led but low-tier. And Create is cross-cutting, landing at Tier 0 (a new self-service experience) or Tier 3 (a new CoE capability), so it does not sit on one rung. The labels describe how AI fits a task; the tiers describe where it is delivered. They line up, rather than being the same thing.

    Where the processes overlap

    Many activities touch more than one process. This view shows where, and suggests one home for each so the work is not owned twice. The suggested home is a starting point for each process owner to confirm. These overlaps are based on the starting activities, so they do not reflect activities you add or rename. The hours on each process page are not reduced by what is shown here.

    How to read this

    A chip shows the process, the activity and its hours per week in the starting view, or your own hours if you changed them. The outlined chip is the suggested home. Hours in different processes are never added together, because some of the same work appears in more than one place. Three kinds of overlap are shown. Same work in two places needs one owner. A hand-off is work that ends in one process and starts in the next, so the two sides should match. A shared layer is repeated on purpose in every process.
